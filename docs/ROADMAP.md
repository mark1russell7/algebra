# Roadmap

This file is the plan of algebra. The Status page of the site reads it, and the tracker of the project mirrors it.

Each item writes its prediction before its code: the claims, how each claim is measured, its base and its noise.

| ID | Item | Status | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Expression documents | done 2026-11-02 (#8) | The package `@mark1russell7/expression`. The coeval kinds of an expression: numbers, variables, the field operations, powers, roots, `min`, `max` and comparisons. Each node keeps a summary monoid: free variables, degree, bounds and cost. A zod schema and a generator of random expressions for property tests. |
| A2 | Exact rationals | done 2026-11-03 | Rationals over `bigint`, always in lowest terms. The start is the rationals of `@mark1russell7/quantity` in systems: they move here, and quantity imports them (systems #284). Property tests of the field laws. The time claim holds: each of 32 cases of the tests of quantity is within 2× of the old rationals (bench run 41647641389). The root `root(a, k)` and `integerRoot(n, k)` (0.1.1) give the exact verb a claim for each rational root. |
| A3 | The float evaluator | done 2026-11-02 (#30) | An expression compiles once to a JavaScript function of its variables, with no `eval` at run time. It also gives a bound of its rounding error from the summary of each node. The verb `float` of the expression kinds is the evaluator, and `compileFloat` gives the function. Time claim: within 1.5× of a hand-written function on the corpus of A12. `packages/expression-bench` and `bench-expression.yml` time it against a written function of each document (#49). Run 41716693461 refutes the time claim: the median ratio is 65.6, and each of the 64 cases is above 1.5. The verb `static` gives the bound over a box of the variables with no point. `compileTape` gives the same value and bound from a flat list of instructions. Run 41895886738 keeps the pairs `ft` and `wt` in `packages/expression-bench/results/2026-11-04/`. The tape over the closures has a median of 0.765, and the closures win only at 7 nodes or fewer. The tape over the written function with a bound has a median of 3.882. Runs 41912567142 and 41912596070 test the threshold on 176 new cases, kept in `packages/expression-bench/results/`. At 7 nodes or fewer, the closures win in 36 of 38 cases. At 15 nodes or more, the tape wins in 122 of 123 cases. From 8 to 14 nodes, the result is a tie. `compileFloat` uses the tape above 10 nodes, and `compileClosures` keeps the closures by name. These records selected the value 10. Runs 41948999649 and 41949047378 test the rule on 176 other cases: 3.98 percent of the cases are wrong, the geometric mean over the closures is 0.708, the worst ratio is 1.147, and the median compile ratio is 1.031. The share of wrong cases in `small` alone is 12.5 percent, near the line. |
| A4 | Interval and ball arithmetic | done 2026-11-02 (#35) | Intervals with outward rounding (one step of `nextafter` on each bound), and balls (a midpoint and a radius). The property: the interval contains the exact value of A2 at each point of the corpus. The verb `static` gives the interval and the float bound for a box of the variables. |
| A5 | Exact geometric predicates | in progress | Orientation, in-circle and segment intersection. A float filter with a stated error bound answers first, and the exact evaluator answers the rest. A bench against `robust-predicates` on random and near-degenerate inputs. The first users are packing and highways. The package `@mark1russell7/predicates` has `orient2d`, `incircle` and `classifySegments`, each with the stage that decided it. The exact stage uses the dyadic numbers, which moved from the census to this package. On 100 000 random points, the filter decides each call of each predicate (#41). `packages/predicates-bench` and `bench-predicates.yml` time it against `robust-predicates` (#44). An adaptive stage over float expansions now answers before the exact stage. The package is on npm at 0.1.0. Run 41896000324 of the bench: `ties` is faster (interval 0.58 to 0.71), `near` has no decision (0.93 to 1.23), and `random` is slower (1.14 to 4.02), with `incircle` at approximately 4 times `robust-predicates`. The cause is a product with a subnormal result at each call of the filter, and #97 removes it. Runs 41950165798 and 41950209701 compare one processor, an AMD EPYC 9V45: `incircle` on random points goes from 4.224 to 0.911 times `robust-predicates`, and `orient2d` stays at 1.19. An open result: two fresh processes of the run before #97 keep `orient2d` at approximately 2.8 times in each round, and the cause is not known. Left: the release `0.1.1`, and the report to packing and highways. |
| A6 | The cost and error model | done 2026-11-03 | Each evaluator measures its cost and its error on the corpus. A selector takes a question and a tolerance, and selects the cheapest evaluator that meets the tolerance. It also stitches them: float first, exact only where the filter fails. The selector is itself measured against each fixed choice. The verb `exact` of `@mark1russell7/expression` gives the exact rational. The package `@algebra/evaluator-select` has the questions, the stitched evaluator and the selector. The package `@algebra/evaluator-bench` and the workflow `bench-select.yml` measure the cost and the error of each stage and of the stitched plan in CI. Run 41716746074 is kept in `packages/evaluator-bench/results/2026-11-03/`. The stitched plan is faster than fixed `exact` over all the cases (median ratio 0.277), slower in the family `ill` (1.185), and gives 0 wrong answers. |
| A7 | Arbitrary precision | in progress | Floats with a precision that the caller selects, and correct rounding of the basic operations. The private package `@algebra/bigfloat` has the number type, the correct rounding of `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div`, `sqrt` and `root` in four modes, and the verb `bigfloat` of `@mark1russell7/expression` gives an outward interval at a precision of each call. A variable of the verb can have more bits than a double. The stage `bigfloat` of `@algebra/evaluator-select` doubles the precision up to a stated limit, and it gives no answer past that limit. The workflow `bench-bigfloat.yml` of `@algebra/bigfloat-bench` measures each precision and the stage against the verbs `exact` and `interval` on the corpus of A12. Left: the first run of that workflow, the stage in a default plan after that measure, and a page of A11. A literal with more bits needs a change of the documents, so it stays out. |
| A8 | The symbolic evaluator | in progress | A normal form for polynomials and rational functions, simplification, differentiation and the solution of linear and polynomial equations. The package `@algebra/polynomial` adds the verb `polynomial`: one canonical normal form of a polynomial with exact rational coefficients, simplification by that form, and the partial derivative. A result is an exact equality of polynomial functions, with the error 0. A node that the form cannot hold, or a node past a limit, gives no claim. The verb `rational` adds the normal form of a quotient in lowest terms, by the polynomial gcd. Its field `domain` states the points with no value, so a cancelled factor stays a hole. `solveEquation` gives exact rational roots and, for each irrational root, an isolating interval with rational ends and a stated width. `solveLinear` solves linear systems. The package `@algebra/polynomial-bench` and the workflow `bench-polynomial.yml` are ready. Left: the CI run of the bench. It waits for a heavy slot. |
| A9 | Structures and constructions | done 2026-11-04 (#77, #81) | Monoid, group, ring, field, order and lattice as instances with law tests. Products, quotients, polynomial rings and field extensions. These instances are the first input of the type graph. Shipped: the private package `@algebra/structures`. Each instance is data: its carrier, its operations, its claimed laws and its refuted laws. Laws are data too, and one seeded runner tests each claim. The instances are the rationals, the integers modulo n, the booleans and the doubles. Double add claims no associativity, and its identity is `-0`. The constructions are the product, quotients, polynomial rings and field extensions. Each construction records its inputs, and the runner tests them too. So a forged ideal fails with arguments. A root extension claims a field only when the code decides irreducibility: trial division, the rational root test or a checked Eisenstein prime. Left: no construction of this list. The README of the package states the limits: the rational root test is complete only to degree 3, and the claims over an infinite carrier rest on samples. |
| A10 | vex uses the float evaluator | later | The compiler of vex compiles its formulas through A3, and its domains use the structures of A9. |
| A11 | The site | in progress (#29, #31, #34, #52, A5 explorer) | A live page for each evaluator: type an expression, see each result, its error bound and its cost. The Status page reads this file. The page shows the A1 interval, the A3 float bound, the A4 interval and ball with their widths, the sign that each evaluator proves, and the exact value. A section shows the measured cost of each stage, and `select` gives the plan live in the page. Left: the class bars of the census of A13. |
| A12 | The corpus and the CI bench | done 2026-11-03 (#48, #49) | A generator of expressions: well-conditioned and ill-conditioned, small and large, with near-degenerate geometric inputs. CI runs the bench. Each result keeps its raw records, the losers too. The package `@algebra/expression-corpus` has 8 families with seeded points and the exact condition number of each polynomial point (#48). Its first use is the A3 time claim: `packages/expression-bench` and `bench-expression.yml` (#49). Run 41716693461 is kept in `packages/expression-bench/results/2026-11-03/`. |
| A13 | The base of A5 | done (#94) | The real inputs of packing and highways go through the orientation sign of the A1 bounds. The measure is the count of pairs whose sign the float bound cannot fix, each one confirmed by the exact value. The package `@mark1russell7/predicate-census` is the census. Highways, 7 sets: 183.2 G tests, 24.4 M undecided, 35,865 float-wrong. Of these, class 1 (a defect of the bound) has 0, class 2 (a sign near zero) has 33,815, class 3 (a crossing measure of the bench) has 1,535, and class 4 (an exact tie at the tolerance) has 515. Packing, `pack-select-v2`: 19.8 G tests, 0 undecided, 0 float-wrong. So the float signs have no wrong answer away from a tie on these inputs, and A5 gives robustness at ties and on adversarial inputs. `packages/predicate-census/README.md` has each set. |
